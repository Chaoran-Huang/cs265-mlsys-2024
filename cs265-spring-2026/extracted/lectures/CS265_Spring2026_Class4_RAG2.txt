                     making                        making             1     Introduction
                        the                        the                Transformer models [86] have emerged as the most widely used architecture in applications such
                                                                      as natural language processing and image classification. Transformers have grown larger [5] and
                 registration                      registration       deeper [87], but equipping them with longer context remains difficult [83], since the self-attention
                                                                      module at their heart has time and memory complexity quadratic in sequence length. An important
                                                                      question is whether making attention faster and more memory-efficient can help Transformer models
                          or                       or                 address their runtime and memory challenges for long sequences.
                                                                      Many approximate attention methods have aimed to reduce the compute and memory requirements
                      voting                       voting             of attention. These methods range from sparse-approximation [53, 77] to low-rank approxima-
                                                                      tion [13, 52, 88], and their combinations [3, 9, 96]. Although these methods reduce the compute
                    process                        process            requirements to linear or near-linear in sequence length, many of them do not display wall-clock
                                                                      speedup against standard attention and have not gained wide adoption. One main reason is that
                                                                      they focus on FLOP reduction (which may not correlate with wall-clock speed) and tend to ignore
                       more                        more               overheads from memory access (IO).
